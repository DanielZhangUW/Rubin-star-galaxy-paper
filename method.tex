\section{Data and Methodology}
\label{sec:data-methods}

We first describe Rubin and HST data used in our analysis, followed by a brief review of the Bayesian star-galaxy
separation method proposed in SIL2020. We conclude this Section with a description of detailed method
implementation steps, and discuss the performance of Rubin's default method and our Bayesian star-galaxy
separation method in the next Section. 


\subsection{Rubin Data Preview 2} 
\label{sec:data}

All data collected with the LSST Camera (XXX: REF) during the commissioning of the Rubin Observatory
will be released as Data Preview 2. Coadded deep images and resulting catalogs have been released
in July 2026. These coadded images are based on about 24,000 single-visit images and cover about
3,000 sq. deg. of sky. In our analysis we focus on the COSMOS field because it is both already well observed
by Rubin (to about 1 magnitude shallower limit than anticipated for 10-year LSST) and space-based HST
COSMOS2020/FARMER star and galaxy labels are available \citep{2022ApJS..258...11W}. As a control
sample for code robustness, we also use Rubin data for the Extended Chandra Deep Field South (ECDFS). 

For the COSMOS validation sample, we select Rubin DP2 sources in the rectangular region
\(149.7 < {\rm RA} < 150.7\) and \(1.7 < {\rm Dec} < 2.7\), with
\(16 \le r_{\rm CModel} < 26\). Objects listed in the COSMOS2020/FARMER reference catalog are
matched to DP2 sources using a 0.5 arcsec matching radius. Within the selected region and magnitude
ranges, the DP2 sample contains 269,606 objects, of which 193,586 are matched to the
COSMOS2020/FARMER objects and 76,020 are unmatched (a matching fraction of 72\%). This
somewhat low matching fraction is due to large areas around bright stars that are masked in the
COSMOS2020/FARMER catalog (see Figure~\ref{fig:cosmos-footprint}). 
The matched validation sample contains 8,788 stars and 184,798 galaxies, classified using
\(\texttt{truth\_binary}=1\) for stars and \(\texttt{truth\_binary}=0\) for galaxies. Their number
counts as function of magnitude are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:cosmos-magnitude-distribution}.
The low fraction stars is due to very faint sample limit and high Galactic latitude.

\subsection{The distributions of sources classified using \texttt{extendedness} parameter} 
\label{sec:extendedness-baseline}

Difficulties with star-galaxy separation encountered at the faint end are illustrated in
Figure~\ref{fig:r_extendedness_confusion_cmd}, which compares subsamples classified
using HST ground-truth labels and the default Rubin \texttt{extendedness} binary class in the $r$ band.
As evident, a significant fraction of faint sources within 2 magnitudes from the faint limit are 
misclassified when using default \texttt{extendedness} parameter. Misclassifications have
a more severe impact on stellar sample: only 26\% of ``stars'' selected by \texttt{extendedness}=1
condition are unresolved in HST data. This high level of contamination increases towards the faint
end (see the bottom left panel in Figure~\ref{fig:r_extendedness_confusion_cmd}). As the same time,
the selection completeness for objects unresolved by HST is only 67\%. Due to much higher counts,
the purity and completeness for galaxies are much higher: 98\% and 89\%, respectively.

\subsection{Bayesian Star--Galaxy Separation}
\label{sec:bayesian-sg}

Following SIL2020, we separate the evidence supplied by an
individual morphology measurement from the expected relative abundance of
the two source populations.  Let ${\rm U}$ and ${\rm R}$ denote the unresolved
and resolved hypotheses, and let $d_b$ denote the morphology measurement in
band $b$.  At magnitude $m$, Bayes' theorem gives the posterior odds
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{P({\rm U}\mid d_b,m)}{P({\rm R}\mid d_b,m)}
    =
    \frac{\pi_{\rm U}(m)}{\pi_{\rm R}(m)}
    \frac{\mathcal{L}_{{\rm U},b}(d_b\mid m)}
         {\mathcal{L}_{{\rm R},b}(d_b\mid m)}.
\end{equation*}
Here $\mathcal{L}_{{\rm U},b}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{{\rm R},b}$ are the
class-conditional morphology densities.  Their ratio measures how strongly
the observed morphology favors the unresolved or resolved hypothesis.
The factors $\pi_{\rm U}$ and $\pi_{\rm R}$ are the expected fractions of the
two populations at that magnitude before the morphology of the individual
source is used.  We denote the resulting unresolved-source posterior by
\begin{equation*}
    p_{\rm S}\equiv P({\rm U}\mid d_b,m).
\end{equation*}
The subscript ${\rm S}$ indicates the star-like, unresolved component; it does
not assert that every unresolved source is physically a star.  If
$O_{\rm U/R}$ denotes the posterior odds in the preceding equation, then
\begin{equation*}
    p_{\rm S}=\frac{O_{\rm U/R}}{1+O_{\rm U/R}}.
\end{equation*}

For conditionally independent measurements in a set of bands $\mathcal{B}$,
the morphology likelihood ratios may be combined as
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{B}_{\rm morph}
    =\prod_{b\in\mathcal{B}}
     \frac{\mathcal{L}_{{\rm U},b}(d_b\mid m_b)}
          {\mathcal{L}_{{\rm R},b}(d_b\mid m_b)}.
\end{equation*}
In the multiband implementation, the $r$-band source-count prior is applied
once after the usable per-band morphology evidence has been combined:
\begin{equation*}
    O_{\rm U/R}
    =\frac{\pi_{\rm U}(m_r)}{\pi_{\rm R}(m_r)}
     \mathcal{B}_{\rm morph}.
\end{equation*}
Applying the population prior once prevents the same source-count information
from being counted repeatedly across bands.  The product above treats the
per-band morphology measurements as conditionally independent given the
source class.  A complementary classifier can be included in the same
framework when its calibrated output can be expressed as a compatible
likelihood ratio
\citep{Henrion2011BayesianSG,Slater2020}.

The continuous $p_{\rm S}$ value is the primary output of the method.  When a
binary catalog assignment is required, we use the maximum-posterior rule:
objects with $p_{\rm S}\geq0.5$ are assigned to the unresolved class and
objects with $p_{\rm S}<0.5$ to the resolved class.  This convention is
fixed and used for every binary classification reported in this paper.

\subsection{Implementation of the Probabilistic Morphology Model}
\label{sec:implementation}

\subsubsection{Mixture Model for $\Delta m$ vs. $m_{\rm CModel}$ diagram}
\label{sec:mixture-model}

At a fixed CModel magnitude, the distribution of $\Delta m$ is modeled as a mixture of an unresolved (star) component and $K$ resolved (galaxy) components. The unresolved component is a single Gaussian with mean $\mu_U$, width $\sigma_U$, and weight $w_U$. Each resolved component is a skew-normal distribution with weight $w_{R,k}$, location $\mu_{R,k}$, scale $\sigma_{R,k}$, and shape parameter $\alpha_{R,k}$. The full mixture probability density function is
\begin{equation}
    p(\Delta m \mid m)
    = w_U\,\mathcal{G}(\Delta m;\mu_U,\sigma_U)
    + \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_{R,k}\,\mathcal{S}(\Delta m;\mu_{R,k},\sigma_{R,k},\alpha_{R,k}),
    \label{eq:mixture-model}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{G}$ denotes the Gaussian and $\mathcal{S}$ the skew-normal distribution, and the weights are constrained to sum to unity: $w_U=1-\sum_k w_{R,k}$.

The fitting is performed independently in bins of CModel magnitude. At bright magnitudes ($m_{\rm CModel}<22$), where the resolved population is relatively simple, we use $K=2$ resolved components in bins of width 1--3 mag spanning $15<m_{\rm CModel}<22$. At faint magnitudes ($m_{\rm CModel}\geq22$), where the galaxy morphology distribution is more complex, we use $K=3$ resolved components in finer bins of width 0.5--1 mag spanning $22\leq m_{\rm CModel}<27$. Within each magnitude bin, $\Delta m$ values in the range $(-0.5,1.5)$ are histogrammed into 300 bins and normalized to a probability density. The mixture model is fit to these histograms using bounded least-squares optimization with Poisson-like uncertainties $\sigma_i=\sqrt{N_i+1}/(N\,\delta)$, where $N_i$ is the count in bin $i$, $N$ is the total number of sources in the magnitude slice, and $\delta$ is the histogram bin width.

This fitting procedure is carried out in three steps. In the first step, all mixture parameters are fit independently in each magnitude bin. This free fit works well at bright magnitudes, where unresolved and resolved sources are clearly separated in $\Delta m$. However, at faint magnitudes ($m_{\rm CModel}\gtrsim24$), photometric noise broadens the unresolved Gaussian until it becomes degenerate with the resolved components. The fitter can no longer reliably distinguish the two populations, causing the unresolved Gaussian to absorb flux from the galaxy distribution. This inflates the inferred star fraction at faint magnitudes.

To prevent this degeneracy, we impose smooth constraints on the unresolved component. In the second step, the per-bin star parameters from step 1 are replaced with smooth functions of magnitude: $\mu_U$ is fixed to a single value (the median across all bins, typically $\approx0$ in each band); $\sigma_U(m)$ is fit with the photometric error model from B.~\citet{Sesar2007} (their eq. 5),
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_U(m)=\sqrt{\sigma_0^2+a^2\cdot10^{b(m-25)}},
    \label{eq:unresolved-width}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_0$, $a$, and $b$ are free parameters; and $w_U(m)$ is modeled as a linear fit to $\log_{10}(w_U)$ versus magnitude, using only bins brighter than $m_{\rm CModel}=24$ where the free fit is reliable. This smooth model is then extrapolated to fainter magnitude bins. In the third step, with the unresolved component ($\mu_U$, $\sigma_U$, $w_U$) fixed to the smooth curves from step 2, only the resolved skew-normal parameters are re-fit in each magnitude bin. The resolved weights are rescaled to sum to $(1-w_U)$ so that the total mixture remains a proper probability density. The effectiveness of this constrained fitting procedure is demonstrated in Figure~\ref{fig:sg-count-ratio}, which compares the galaxy-to-star count ratio $\log_{10}(N_{\rm gal}/N_{\rm star})$ as a function of magnitude for models with and without the smooth constraints. The unconstrained model exhibits a spurious turnover at faint magnitudes, while the constrained model recovers the expected monotonically increasing ratio in agreement with the COSMOS2020 external truth labels.

From the fitted model, the probability that a source is unresolved is computed as the ratio of the unresolved component to the total mixture density at the observed $(\Delta m,m_{\rm CModel})$:
\begin{equation}
    p_S=\frac{w_U\,\mathcal{G}(\Delta m;\mu_U,\sigma_U)}{p(\Delta m\mid m)}.
    \label{eq:ps-posterior}
\end{equation}
The model parameters are looked up by the CModel magnitude bin in which each source falls. The quality of the fits is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:mixture-slices}, which shows the mixture model overlaid on the observed $\Delta m$ histograms in each magnitude bin, and in Figure~\ref{fig:mixture-map}, which shows the 2D data, model, and residual maps in the $\Delta m$ vs. $m_{\rm CModel}$ plane.

\subsubsection{Magnitude-dependent Source-count Priors}
\label{sec:sg-priors}

The normalization parameters of the best-fit mixture provide a
magnitude-dependent prior for the relative abundance of unresolved and
resolved sources. Here ``prior'' means the expected class ratio at a given
magnitude before the morphology measurement of an individual source is
considered. It is therefore a population term, rather than an alternative
choice of the $p_{\rm S}=0.5$ catalog-assignment boundary. In the notation of
Section~\ref{sec:bayesian-sg}, the fitted priors are
\begin{equation}
    \pi_{\rm U}(m)=w_{\rm U}(m),
    \qquad
    \pi_{\rm R}(m)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_{{\rm R},k}(m)
                  =1-w_{\rm U}(m).
    \label{eq:mixture-priors}
\end{equation}
The corresponding resolved-to-unresolved count ratio is
\begin{equation}
    R_{\rm R/U}(m)
    \equiv\frac{N_{\rm R}(m)}{N_{\rm U}(m)}
    =\frac{\pi_{\rm R}(m)}{\pi_{\rm U}(m)}
    =\frac{1-w_{\rm U}(m)}{w_{\rm U}(m)}.
    \label{eq:count-prior}
\end{equation}
When the unresolved and resolved populations are identified with stars and
galaxies, respectively, $R_{\rm R/U}$ is the galaxy-to-star count ratio shown
in Figure~\ref{fig:sg-count-ratio}. Equation~\ref{eq:ps-posterior} already
incorporates this prior through $w_{\rm U}$ and the total mixture density. The
prior must therefore be applied only once; multiplying an additional count
ratio into Equation~\ref{eq:ps-posterior} would double-count the same
population information.

For comparison, the HST-based external COSMOS ratio is measured in the same
magnitude bins as
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{R}_{\rm G/S}^{\rm HST}(m_j)
    =\frac{N_{{\rm gal},{\rm HST}}(m_j)}
           {N_{\star,{\rm HST}}(m_j)},
    \label{eq:reference-count-ratio}
\end{equation}
using the matched COSMOS classifications described in
Section~\ref{sec:data} \citep{Leauthaud2007COSMOSACS,Robin2007COSMOSStars,
Weaver2022}. Because these labels do not enter the mixture fit, the HST curve
is an external empirical check on the inferred abundance trend. The agreement
of the constrained curve with the overall rise of the HST ratio supports the
use of the fitted normalization as a prior. The statistical uncertainty of
this ratio increases in bins containing few reference stars.

TRILEGAL provides a complementary prediction for the stellar surface density
along the COSMOS line of sight \citep{Girardi2005TRILEGAL,
DalTio2022TRILEGAL}. Because TRILEGAL predicts stellar counts rather than
galaxy counts, we compare it directly with the stellar surface density in the
COSMOS reference sample. In magnitude bin $m_j$, the observed stellar surface
density is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{n}_{\star}^{\rm COSMOS}(m_j)
    =\frac{N_{\star,{\rm COSMOS}}(m_j)}
           {A_{\rm eff}\,\Delta m_j},
    \label{eq:cosmos-star-counts}
\end{equation}
where $A_{\rm eff}$ is the effective matched area and $\Delta m_j$ is the bin
width. The COSMOS and TRILEGAL counts are compared using the same photometric
band, extinction convention, magnitude bins, source selection, and
solid-angle normalization. This direct comparison avoids introducing an
empirical galaxy numerator into the TRILEGAL diagnostic. The posterior
probabilities used in this work retain the empirical mixture prior of
Equation~\ref{eq:mixture-priors}; neither the HST count ratio nor the TRILEGAL
stellar-count prediction is used to modify the posterior or the fixed
$p_{\rm S}=0.5$ catalog-assignment boundary.
