\section{Performance of the Bayesian Classifier}
\label{sec:performance}

\subsection{Validation Metrics}
\label{sec:validation-metrics}

We evaluate classification performance using the COSMOS2020/FARMER
validation sample described in Section~\ref{sec:data}.  The one-to-one
0.5-arcsec match contains 193,586 objects, including 8,788 stars and
184,798 galaxies.  Individual comparisons may contain fewer objects when
one or more required morphology or color measurements are not finite.

For either class treated as positive, completeness is defined as
\begin{equation}
    C = \frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}},
\end{equation}
while contamination is
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{cont}}
    = \frac{\mathrm{FP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}}.
\end{equation}
Purity is \(1-f_{\mathrm{cont}}\).  For the ROC curves, the false-positive
rate is instead defined as
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{FPR}
    = \frac{\mathrm{FP}}{\mathrm{FP}+\mathrm{TN}},
\end{equation}
and is therefore distinct from contamination of the selected sample.
The area under the ROC curve (AUC) characterizes ranking performance
independently of a particular classification threshold.


\subsection{Morphology-only ROC Performance}
\label{sec:morphology-roc}

The Rubin r-band extendedness classifier corresponds to a threshold in
\begin{equation}
    \Delta m_r
    = m_{{\rm PSF},r} - m_{{\rm CModel},r}.
\end{equation}
Sweeping this threshold traces the full morphology baseline, while the
Rubin catalog default, \(\Delta m_r=0.016\) mag, defines a single operating
point.  The stored V9 \(p_{{\rm S},r}\) values provide the corresponding
prior-informed probabilistic ranking.

On the common sample of 193,516 objects with finite r-band morphology,
the AUC is 0.871 for the extendedness threshold sweep and 0.901 for the
stored V9 probabilistic score.  At the default extendedness operating
point, stellar completeness is 0.722 and stellar-sample contamination is
0.751, while galaxy completeness is 0.897 and galaxy purity is 0.985.

\subsection{Magnitude-dependent Single-band Performance}
\label{sec:single-band-performance}

Figure~\ref{fig:single-band-performance} compares the fixed operating point
\(p_{{\rm S},r}=0.5\) with the default Rubin extendedness classification.
The left and right columns show star-selected and galaxy-selected samples,
respectively, while the rows show completeness, contamination, and purity
in three r-band magnitude intervals.

At \(16\leq r<24\), both methods perform well; the probabilistic
star-selected sample has completeness 0.878 and purity 0.975.  At
\(24\leq r<25\), the probabilistic selection reduces stellar-sample
contamination from 0.682 to 0.442 and increases purity from 0.318 to
0.558, at the cost of reducing completeness from 0.582 to 0.268.
At \(25\leq r<26\), no source in the evaluated sample satisfies
\(p_{{\rm S},r}\geq0.5\), reflecting the strongly galaxy-dominated
posterior-score distribution at the faint end.  In this final bin,
stellar-sample contamination and purity are undefined because the
probabilistic classifier selects no star-like objects.

These results illustrate that a single fixed threshold does not provide
the same completeness--purity tradeoff at all magnitudes.  Because
\(p_{\rm S}\) is continuous, its operating threshold can instead be
chosen for the requirements of a particular science analysis.

\subsection{Multiband and Color Performance}
\label{sec:multiband-color-performance}

For the current multiband morphology diagnostic, the no-prior morphology
log-likelihood ratios from the available bands are summed and the r-band
count prior is applied once.  We compare r, gri, and ugrizy morphology
scores on a common complete-case sample within each magnitude interval.
The color-enhanced score adds the logit of the existing five-color Random
Forest score to the ugrizy morphology score.  Because the Random Forest
output has not yet been demonstrated to be a calibrated likelihood ratio,
the combined quantity is treated as a ranking score rather than as a
calibrated posterior probability.

The current star-positive ROC diagnostic is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:multiband-color-roc}.  The ugrizy morphology score has
higher AUC than the r-only score at \(16\leq r<24\)
(\(0.978\) versus \(0.965\)) and at \(24\leq r<25\)
(\(0.777\) versus \(0.759\)), but not at \(25\leq r<26\)
(\(0.721\) versus \(0.734\)).  The gri score is also slightly below the
r-only result in the two faint intervals, indicating that adding noisy
bands does not necessarily improve ranking.

In the current diagnostic, adding the five-color score to the ugrizy
morphology score increases the star-positive AUC from 0.978 to 0.986,
from 0.777 to 0.815, and from 0.721 to 0.779 in the three magnitude
intervals, respectively.  These comparisons use the same objects within
each panel, so the differences are not produced by changes in sample
membership.  The color-related AUC values will be treated as final only
after the Random Forest evaluation is confirmed to use held-out or
out-of-fold predictions.

\subsection{Color-space Diagnostic}
\label{sec:color-space-diagnostic}

Figure~\ref{fig:method-color-space} shows the output classifications for
the DP2 subset with the finite morphology and color measurements required
by the combined ugrizy-plus-color score.  These are method-assigned labels,
not COSMOS2020/FARMER truth labels.  Objects with positive combined score
are shown as method-unresolved points over density contours for the
method-resolved population.

The \(16\leq r<25\) column contains 5,855 method-unresolved and 106,740
method-resolved objects, whereas the \(25\leq r<26\) column contains only
180 and 25,707, respectively.  The sparse faint unresolved locus is
consistent with a strongly galaxy-dominated population and reduced
morphology and color information near the survey limit.  Because the
combined classifier uses these colors as input, this figure is descriptive
rather than an independent quantitative validation.

The continuous probabilistic scores permit operating thresholds to be
adapted to the completeness and purity requirements of different science
applications.  The validated r-band results show improved morphology
ranking relative to the default extendedness classifier, although a fixed
\(p_{\rm S}=0.5\) threshold becomes overly conservative for selecting
faint stars.  The current multiband diagnostics show improvements in some,
but not all, magnitude intervals, while the color-enhanced score shows
promising ranking gains that require independent evaluation and calibration.
A future value-added catalog can provide the continuous \(p_{\rm S}\) or
\(p_{\rm G}\) scores together with their prior, model-version, and
calibration provenance.
