First V12-family fit to the unchanged D_griz distribution, 2026-11-06

Purpose
Fit a probability mixture to the existing D_griz measurements and predict the
completeness/contamination of the ORIGINAL D_griz < threshold selection.

Data and boundary
The original D_griz = sqrt(sum_b ((Delta_m_b-mu_b)/sigma_b)^2) is loaded from
the frozen V12 score table. It is never rescaled, replaced, or recomputed by
the fitter. Population measurements are object_id, r_Sersic and D_griz.
No HST class, Gaia class, per-object size, photometric error, or official
extendedness column enters the fit or numerical model selection.
The accepted reference IDs supply membership, as authorized previously.
HST class values are opened only in evaluate.py, after model freezing.

Model adaptation
This is the V12 density family applied to a new nonnegative variable, D.
It is a new D-distribution fit, not reuse of Delta-m parameters. The narrow U
core is Johnson SU, parameterized by its D-space mode/local width. U also has
a positive lognormal wing shifted from the core mode. R has two continuous
skew-normal templates. All densities are normalized on [0, infinity).
U and R weights are nonnegative and add to one. D-space core parameters have
no role in recalculating the individual-band cores or the original D score.

Fitting sequence
1. Fit shared U-core curves and continuous R templates to Rubin parent D data.
2. Hold template shapes fixed; fit reference population fractions from D and
   r magnitude, without opening HST classes.
3. Freeze the core and reference fraction; fit U-wing and R-shape parameters.
Three numerical starts are compared by unlabeled held-out binned likelihood.
The held-out split uses object-ID hash mod 5. Fit and evaluation magnitudes
are 18 <= r_Sersic < 26; the displayed main panels span 23--26 in 0.5-mag bins.

Numerical details
The likelihood integrates component CDFs over D bins; it does not sample only
bin centers. Magnitude cells are 0.125 mag wide. D bins are 0.1 wide below 10,
then logarithmically spaced up to 8192 with an explicit infinite overflow bin.
ALL finite D values are retained. Stable CDF/survival differences preserve
small tail masses. Analytical gradients pass independent finite differences.
PDF/CDF derivative checks and component normalization checks pass.
Model expectations average over the actual magnitude distribution in each
displayed slice, with a numerical resolution convergence check.

Reading the plots
01: black observed counts, blue U, orange R, green sum. Only D<=10 is shown;
    the number of observed objects beyond D=10 is explicitly printed.
02: blue CC is counted with HST labels; orange CC is predicted by the fitted
    distribution. Gray shows the earlier core-template prediction before
    the reference wing/R-shape refit. Circles mark D<3; squares mark D<5.
    Blue and orange markers use exactly the same numerical D threshold.
03: HST star/galaxy histograms are overlaid only after parameter freezing.
04: full-range fit including large D; counts use equal logarithmic bins.

Prediction definitions
U_selected(t) = sum_i w_U(m_i) F_U(t | m_i).
R_selected(t) = sum_i [1-w_U(m_i)] F_R(t | m_i).
Predicted completeness = U_selected / sum_i w_U(m_i).
Predicted contamination = R_selected / (U_selected + R_selected).
Measured completeness and contamination use the corresponding HST counts.
The model and HST denominators therefore test the inferred population and
component shapes together; no HST star count is substituted into predictions.

Initial finding
The total D distribution and unlabeled held-out likelihood improve after
the wing/R refit. However the predicted CC curves show systematic mismatch
with HST, especially at high completeness and at faint magnitudes.
For D<3 in 24--24.5, predicted C=44.7%, Q=4.2%; HST C=36.9%, Q=4.7%.
For D<3 in 24.5--25, predicted C=39.0%, Q=19.8%; HST C=34.1%, Q=27.3%.
For D<3 in 25.5--26, predicted C=28.6%, Q=67.7%; HST C=21.0%, Q=80.3%.
The component check shows an overestimated U peak in the intermediate/faint
bins and insufficient R near the low-D overlap. This first fit does not yet
provide calibrated CC predictions. It is an experimental result for review,
not a replacement for the adopted paper model or its measured D CC curves.
